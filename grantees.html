Our grantees

Important problems, and the people working to solve them

The organizations we support have big problems to tackle and the skills to use AI on them. What they were missing was access to the computing power to do it.

Agriculture · India and Africa

Digital Green

Digital Green helps smallholder farmers get better agricultural advice through technology. Its AI assistant, Farmer.Chat, answers farmers' questions about crops and livestock in their own languages. It now serves 2.1 million farmers, with a goal of 4 million by March 2027.

Digital Green's team used our computers for early tests of open AI models that understand farmers' voices and photos of their crops. Their own image model now identifies crops with 96.5% accuracy, compared with 87.2% for a leading commercial app, at a fraction of the cost per question. Tested on more than 2,700 real recordings of farmers speaking Hindi, Telugu, and Odia, open voice models matched or beat commercial ones on noisy, real-world audio.

Next, they want to extend crop diagnosis to many more crops and tune voice models for real field conditions. That work needs even more powerful computers, which is one reason we're upgrading ours.

Conservation · Referred by the Bezos Earth Fund

Revive & Restore

Revive & Restore uses biotechnology to protect wildlife, including the genetic rescue of endangered species. On our cluster, its team is applying machine learning to genome data from animals to predict which species are most at risk of extinction.

The goal is a practical tool that conservation managers can use in the field. The work means analyzing very large volumes of genetic data, which isn't possible without serious computing power.

City climate policy · Government

NYC Office of Management & Budget

New York City's Office of Management and Budget leads the city's Climate Budgeting process, which makes sure emissions are part of how the city decides what to invest in.

The team built two computer-vision tools that read footage from the city's roughly 1,000 public traffic cameras. The tools worked, but they had only ever run on a single personal computer. On our cluster the team can improve them and run them citywide, giving New York far more detailed emissions data for its climate and transportation policy.

Coming soon

Climate and conservation projects from the Bezos Earth Fund

Through our partnership with the Bezos Earth Fund, teams from its AI Grand Challenge who need computing power are referred to us. Projects on the way include using computer vision to find coral reefs that can survive a warming ocean, predicting how much carbon ocean-based approaches can remove from the atmosphere, and mapping deep-sea ecosystems with AI.
