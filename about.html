About us

The social sector deserves AI infrastructure of its own

AI is advancing faster than any technology in recent memory, and it's starting to transform science, medicine, and public services. The organizations working on society's hardest problems should be able to use it too.

The Foundation for Civic AI exists to show what becomes possible when nonprofits and public agencies have free, accessible AI infrastructure. We provide computing power and the expertise to use it, and we share what we learn so others can build on it.

Our approach

How we work

We work with teams ready to build

Our computers are most valuable to organizations with a clear AI project and people who can carry it out. We find them through funders, accelerators, and open applications.

We pair computers with expertise

Hardware alone isn't enough. Our experts help each team plan its work and make the most of its computing time.

We handle the hard parts

Security reviews, legal agreements, and account setup can take longer than the AI work itself. We built a process that lets outside organizations use university computers safely, so your team can focus on the project.

Where we're headed

Growing to meet the need

More organizations are ready to use our computers than we can currently serve. Here's what we're building next.

  • Support for more organizations. More grantees, staff to help them get started, and a larger bench of AI experts.
  • More powerful computers. Newer hardware that keeps up with what today's AI models need.
  • Free, private AI for every nonprofit. Most nonprofits will never build an AI model, but many want to use AI for everyday work like summarizing case notes or translating documents. We're building free AI tools that run on our own hardware and keep none of the data, so legal aid groups, health clinics, and immigrant services organizations can use AI without handing sensitive information to a tech company.
  • An open playbook. We'll publish our legal templates, onboarding process, and lessons learned, so others can build public AI infrastructure of their own.
