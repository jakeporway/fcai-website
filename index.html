Making the power of AI available to the social sector

Recent advances in AI have opened up remarkable possibilities for science, public services, and the communities nonprofits serve. But much of the social sector is being left behind.

We're closing that gap with free, accessible AI infrastructure: the computing power, and the expertise, to put AI to work for the public good.

The challenge

Mission-driven teams can't get the computing power advanced AI needs

Building or improving an AI tool takes serious computing power: racks of specialized machines that most organizations outside big tech never get to use. For nonprofits and public agencies, the obstacles add up.

  • Free cloud credits run out. Tech companies' credit programs are short-lived and designed for startups.
  • Government agencies can't easily buy hardware. Procurement rules and approvals can stall a project for months.
  • The second model is harder than the first. A team that built a promising prototype on a small grant often can't afford to scale it up.
  • University computers are usually off-limits. Most academic computing centers are closed to outside organizations.
  • And the cost is real. Running AI at scale can cost tens of thousands of dollars a year, more than most program budgets allow.

When those barriers hit, projects that could protect endangered species or cut a city's emissions end up on the shelf.

What we do

Computing power and know-how, free for the social sector

01

Free high-performance computing

We run a high-performance computing cluster at Cornell Tech that nonprofits and public agencies can use at no cost, usually for up to a year. It's built from 32 NVIDIA A100 GPUs, the specialized chips AI models are trained on.

02

Expert guidance

Our bench of experienced data scientists and AI engineers helps your team plan the project and use the computers efficiently, so the work goes further and finishes sooner.

03

Private AI tools In development

We're building free AI tools for nonprofits that keep none of your data, so organizations working with sensitive information can use AI with confidence.

How our program works

The bigger picture

A working model for public AI infrastructure

We don't just want to run a service. Our goal is to prove a model others can adopt: shared AI infrastructure, run for the public good and open to the organizations that need it.

We're documenting everything we learn, from legal agreements to onboarding, so universities, funders, and governments can build on our work and take it further.

What our grantees are working on

Agriculture

Digital Green

Testing open AI models that help Farmer.Chat, an AI assistant used by 2.1 million smallholder farmers, understand farmers' voices and crop photos from the field.

Conservation

Revive & Restore

Using machine learning on genome data to predict which species are at risk of extinction, and building a tool conservation managers can use in the field.

City climate policy

NYC Office of Management & Budget

Scaling computer-vision tools across the city's roughly 1,000 traffic cameras to measure emissions for New York's climate budget.
